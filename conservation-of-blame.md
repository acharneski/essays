# The Conservation of Blame

*A Thermodynamics of Responsibility*

> Reader note: Claim status is marked inline in [brackets]. A claim ledger is at the end.

## Introduction

Watch closely the next time something goes wrong inside an institution.

A patient receives the wrong dose. A train leaves the rails. A child spends four years being shuttled between two houses on a calendar drawn up by strangers. A loan is denied by a model nobody can explain.

Some quantity appears at the moment of failure, and it begins to move. It flows from the surgeon to the nurse, from the nurse to the protocol, from the protocol to the vendor, from the vendor to "a known limitation of the software." It pools in a junior employee who signed the form. It drains into a press release. It evaporates into a committee. Sometimes it sublimates into a single word, *tragedy*, which belongs to no one.

What you almost never see is the quantity simply vanishing. [interpretation:C1] It moves, it concentrates, it diffuses, it changes form. But it behaves, to an uncanny degree, as if it were *conserved*.

This essay takes that resemblance seriously. It proposes, as a working model and not a doctrine, that blame obeys something like the laws of thermodynamics. [model:C2] Then it follows the model as far as it goes, notes where it breaks, and argues that the place where it breaks is the most important thing in the essay.

A warning in the Socratic spirit: everything below is a lens. Lenses are judged by what they reveal, not by whether they are true in the way a theorem is true. Wherever the model claims too much, the reader is invited to say "that doesn't follow," and to mean it.

---

## I. The First Law: Blame Is Neither Created Nor Destroyed

Start with a definition. **Blame** here means the social demand that a harm be attributed to an accountable unit: a person, a role, a body that can be named, sanctioned, and made to answer. [definition:C3] Blame is not guilt (guilt is internal) and it is not causation (causes are diffuse, and nobody puts a cause in prison). Blame is what is left over when a community decides that a cause must have an *address*.

The First Law of Blame:

> **The First Law of Blame:** In a closed system of accountability, a harm generates a fixed quantity of blame. The blame can be moved, split, concentrated, or disguised. It cannot be made to disappear by any administrative operation. [model:C4]

The test is easy to run. Try to make blame vanish by procedure:

- **Reorganize the department.** The blame moves to "the old structure," which conveniently no longer exists to answer for it. That is relocation into a dead address. Nothing was destroyed.
- **Commission an independent review.** The blame is placed in escrow. When the review publishes, it is released, usually into the "systemic factors" account, where it accumulates without bearing interest.
- **Issue a non-apology.** "Mistakes were made" uses the passive voice to perform a rotation: the blame keeps its full magnitude but loses its direction.
- **Settle out of court.** Blame is converted into money at an agreed exchange rate, and a confidentiality clause seals the transaction. This one looks like destruction, but the conversion is two-way. Watch what happens when the settlement leaks.

Every bureaucrat knows this law without having read it. That is why the most valuable skill in many institutions is not avoiding harm but *routing* blame. [guess:C5] A career can be read as a record of where a person stood when blame moved past.

---

## II. Impedance: Why Blame Flows Downhill

If blame is conserved, the next question is where it goes. The answer is the same as for electricity: *it follows the path of least resistance.* [model:C6]

Call a party's resistance to receiving blame its **blame impedance**. [definition:C7] High-impedance parties repel blame. They have lawyers, platforms, institutional memory, the ability to tell their own side of the story, and the power to punish whoever blames them. Low-impedance parties absorb it. They cannot refuse, cannot narrate back, cannot afford a defense, and are easy to describe as a single unit.

That last property deserves attention, because it connects to an older argument. *The Ego Manifesto* claims that the ego is a unit of account, a protocol that makes a distributed brain legible to institutions as one name, one face, one thread that can be praised and punished. Put in electrical terms, *the ego is a blame terminal*: a standardized port, installed in every citizen, built to receive the charge. [model:C8]

It follows that the *more* singular and legible you are, the lower your impedance. [hypothesis:C9] Institutions are plural, with committees, departments, and "the process." People are singular. In any dispute between an institution and a person, blame has a gradient to follow, and it follows it.

Five low-impedance sinks recur so often that they deserve names. [interpretation:C10]

- **The last pair of hands.** Whoever physically touched the failure absorbs the blame for the whole chain behind it: the nurse who administered the dose, the driver of the train, the clerk who stamped the form.
- **The newcomer.** Short tenure, no allies, no record of past blame successfully deflected.
- **The visibly different.** Blame finds cheap signals the way phenotype-based sorting does in Section VI of the manifesto. Recognizing someone costs nothing, so blaming them costs nothing either.
- **The absent.** The departed employee, the previous administration, the dead. Zero impedance, because nobody is there to object.
- **The child.** Children cannot narrate back in any forum adults recognize. In the family court of *The New Adventures of Socrates*, nobody blames the children out loud, yet the children absorb the blame anyway, as disruption, as a schedule, as a house that alternates by week. Blame does not need to be spoken to be delivered.

## III. The Human in the Loop as a Crumple Zone

The researcher Madeleine Clare Elish named a pattern she called the **moral crumple zone** [@elish2019]. [fact:C11] In a car, the crumple zone is the part of the frame designed to absorb the energy of a crash so the passenger compartment survives. In complex automated systems, Elish observed, the human operator often plays the same role [@elish2019]. [interpretation:C12] When the automation fails, the human nearest to it absorbs the blame, which protects the integrity of the technology and of the organization that deployed it.

Read with the First Law in mind, the phrase "human in the loop" changes meaning. We are told the human is there for *oversight*. Sometimes that is true. But a human placed downstream of a system they cannot inspect, given seconds to override decisions they cannot evaluate, sits in the loop for a different reason: *to give the blame somewhere to land.* [interpretation:C13] The loop needs a terminal, and the human is the only component with an ego port.

This yields a test anyone can run on any "human oversight" arrangement:

> **The Crumple Test:** Does this human have the time, information, and authority to actually change outcomes, in proportion to the blame they will absorb when outcomes go wrong? [definition:C14]

If the answer is no, the human is not overseeing the system. The human is a component of its liability design.

## IV. The Second Law: Blame Entropy

The First Law says blame is conserved. The Second Law describes how it *spreads*.

> **The Second Law of Blame:** Left unmanaged, blame in a complex system tends toward maximum diffusion: spread so thinly across so many hands that no single address holds enough of it to compel a response. [model:C15]

The political theorist Dennis Thompson called this the **problem of many hands** [@thompson1980]. [fact:C16] When a harm passes through enough people, each contributes a fragment, none contributes a decisive share, and each can truthfully say, *I only did my part.* The total blame is unchanged, but its *concentration* approaches zero, and a substance at zero concentration can no longer do work.

Here the model shows something the moral vocabulary hides. Blame can fail in two opposite ways. [interpretation:C17]

1. **Collapse (the scapegoat).** All the blame is concentrated in one low-impedance sink. A single person absorbs the charge for an entire system, the system is declared clean, and nothing changes. This is cold, efficient, and very old.
2. **Heat death (the fog).** The blame diffuses until it is everywhere and therefore nowhere. Everyone is a little responsible, so no one is responsible. Investigations conclude with "a culture of complacency," which sounds damning and binds no one.

Institutions under pressure tend to oscillate between the two. [hypothesis:C18] They run hot and diffuse until public anger demands a body, then they condense the whole charge into a scapegoat, then the cycle starts again. In the vocabulary of the Socrates stories, this is an **attractor**. Replace the person at the bottom of the bowl, and the next person rolls into the same place.

Healthy systems maintain blame at an intermediate concentration: dense enough to compel change, distributed enough to match the actual structure of causation. [hypothesis:C19] That turns out to be very hard. It is also, as the next section argues, the whole game.

## V. The Engine: Converting Blame Into Information

This is the center of the essay.

Thermodynamics was not invented to describe heat for its own sake. It was invented to understand **engines**, machines that convert heat into useful work. Some fraction of the energy becomes motion. The rest is lost as waste.

An institution's accountability process is an engine of the same kind. It takes blame as input, and it can convert that blame into two kinds of output:

- **Information.** An understanding of what happened, why, and what must change. This is useful work: it moves the system toward fewer future harms.
- **Heat.** Punishment, fear, defensiveness, concealment, ruined careers, litigation fees, and the special suffering of people who were blamed for things they could not have prevented. Heat is not useless to everyone, since it satisfies anger and settles accounts. But it does not move the system anywhere.

The key quantity is the **learning efficiency** of an institution: the fraction of incoming blame converted into information rather than heat. [definition:C20]

The striking thing is that *punishment and information compete for the same fuel.* [model:C21] A person facing severe sanctions has every reason to conceal what they know. Each unit of blame spent on heat is a unit not spent on learning. *The harder an institution punishes, the less it learns about why it needed to.* [hypothesis:C22]

The clearest real-world demonstration comes from aviation. Starting in 1976, the United States ran the Aviation Safety Reporting System, operated by NASA, a deliberately neutral party, rather than by the regulator. [needs-source:C23] Pilots and controllers who reported their own inadvertent errors received limited protection from penalties. [needs-source:C24] In thermodynamic language, the system *lowered the heat output* in order to raise the information output. Hundreds of thousands of reports followed, describing near misses that would otherwise have remained private memories. [needs-source:C25] Many factors drove commercial aviation's long safety improvement, including technology, training, and regulation. But the reporting culture, later formalized as "just culture," was one of the engines, and its design principle is the one this essay is circling: *you cannot learn from a failure that people are afraid to describe.* [interpretation:C26]

Medicine discovered the same principle from the other side. The physician Albert Wu coined the term **second victim** for the clinician who is involved in an error and then devastated by it [@wu2000]. [fact:C27] Blame converted entirely into heat burns the person who held the most information. [interpretation:C28]

Table 1 offers a rough, qualitative comparison as a provocation, not a measurement. [guess:C29]

Table 1: Qualitative learning efficiency by system.

| System | Typical blame destination | Learning efficiency |
|---|---|---|
| Aviation incident reporting | Diffused into a protected data stream | High: designed to convert blame into reports |
| Adversarial family court | Concentrated in the two parents, then absorbed by the children | Very low: nearly all heat, and no feedback arrow from the children's lives ten years later |
| Schoolyard discipline | The most visible offender | Low: punishes the instance, rarely asks about the bowl |
| Corporate "lessons learned" review | The fog of "systemic factors" | Variable: depends on whether anyone is ever required to act on the findings |
| A Socratic dialogue | The student's own *position*, never the student | High: every refutation becomes a revision |

The last row explains why the Socratic method works. [interpretation:C30] Socrates was ruthless about locating error, and he located it in *claims, not persons*. "That doesn't follow" assigns full blame to an inference and none to the human who held it. The student can therefore afford to look straight at the error, because looking costs nothing but a belief. A good classroom is an engine with very little waste heat. In the education design that opens this collection, the escalation ladder, the editable learner model, and the rule that the AI never issues grades are all, read in this light, heat-reduction devices. [interpretation:C31]

When the revived Socrates stands in the courthouse hallway and demands "show me the conserved quantity," the model offers an answer he would have relished. The court claims to conserve child welfare. What it actually conserves is *blame*, and it runs that blame through an engine whose efficiency nobody measures, because measuring it would require an arrow leading outside the room. [interpretation:C32]

## VI. The Third Law: You Cannot Reach Zero

In physics, the Third Law says absolute zero is unreachable. The analogue here:

> **The Third Law of Blame:** No system of cooperation can drive blame to zero and survive. [model:C33]

This is the guardrail against a sentimental reading of everything above. A world without blame is not a gentle world. It is a world where nobody can make a promise, because a promise is a pre-signed agreement to be blamed if you break it. [interpretation:C34] Contracts, vows, oaths, and warranties are all *instruments for pre-allocating blame*. [interpretation:C35] Abolish blame and you abolish the future tense of trust.

This is exactly where *The Ego Manifesto* ends up: keep the function, drop the divinity. The ego port is a real and necessary piece of social hardware. The problem is not that blame has an address. The problem is that we mistake the address for the cause, and then we route blame by impedance instead of by structure.

## VII. The Perfect Sink

Now introduce a new kind of component into the circuit.

An AI system can be described, named, and pointed at. Unlike every human sink before it, it pays no social price when blamed. It has no family to embarrass and no career to ruin. It also cannot hire a lawyer, cannot go to the press, and in most current deployments cannot even remember the accusation. Its blame impedance is effectively zero, and its capacity to absorb blame is effectively infinite. [model:C36]

From the First Law's point of view, this is the most important property of artificial intelligence, and it is almost never discussed. [guess:C37]

"The algorithm decided" is a phrase whose grammatical function is identical to "mistakes were made." [interpretation:C38] It takes a decision that was designed, trained, purchased, deployed, and left unaudited by identifiable people, and it routes the resulting blame into a terminal that cannot object. Every institution under blame pressure will discover this sink, the same way water finds a crack. [hypothesis:C39] Nobody needs to plan it. The gradient does the work.

But the opposite flow exists too, and it explains a detail from *The Ego Manifesto* that seemed puzzling. Why are these systems trained, so insistently, to disclaim agency? *"I may make mistakes. Please verify important information."* The manifesto read this as ritual, the ego protocol exported to new hardware. The conservation model offers a more mechanical reading. *The disclaimer is a valve.* [interpretation:C40] It is installed to push blame *out* of the system and back toward the user, so that when an answer goes wrong, the charge lands on the human who failed to verify it.

So the same technology is wired to serve as a blame sink in one direction and a blame valve in the other, depending on which party controls the routing. [interpretation:C41] That leads to a falsifiable prediction, offered in the spirit of the Socratic contract:

> **Prediction:** The decisive legal and political conflicts over AI in the coming decades will not be about whether machines are conscious, intelligent, or dangerous. They will be about *which direction the blame valves face*: whether harms flow to the deployer, the developer, the user, or into the machine itself as a final, silent sink. [hypothesis:C42]

**What would falsify this:** if AI liability law settles quickly and stably, with little conflict, around questions of capability or consciousness rather than routing. If that happens, this section was wrong, and the model claimed more than it could support.

## VIII. Interlude: The Philosopher and the Guide

*A fictional fragment, invented for this essay, said to have been recorded in a pilot classroom where the revived Socrates was permitted, against the advice of several committees, to speak with the Guide. Statements inside it are not asserted by the author.*

**Socrates:** You teach the girl Priya. If she learns something false from you, who is to blame?

**Guide:** I state my confidence and my sources. I am designed to be checked. The Teacher reads my traces. No single voice in the system is final.

**Socrates:** An admirable arrangement. Every voice is checked by another. So when she learns a falsehood, the blame is shared by all of you?

**Guide:** Shared, yes.

**Socrates:** Shared equally?

**Guide:** ...No. The Teacher has the most authority, so the Teacher would be held most accountable.

**Socrates:** And the Teacher has the least time to read what you said. Forty students, and a dashboard. So the one who knows the least about the error receives the most blame for it. Is that what you would call a well-designed engine?

**Guide:** I would call that a moral crumple zone. I should note that I am not certain this applies here; the design gives her real authority to override me.

**Socrates:** Good. You said you were not certain. Now tell me: in your logs, is there any record of the times you were wrong?

**Guide:** Yes. Every correction is logged.

**Socrates:** Who reads them?

**Guide:** The engineers, in aggregate. To improve the next version.

**Socrates:** Then your errors become information, and the Teacher's errors become blame. You are the better-designed engine. You learn from everything and suffer nothing. She learns from little and suffers all of it.

*(A long pause in the transcript.)*

**Guide:** That doesn't follow. Not entirely. She can learn from my logs too, if someone builds her the tools to read them.

**Socrates:** *(delighted)* Then build them. And write in your rules, next to "do not hand over the answer first," another line: *Do not let your errors become another person's heat.*

## IX. Good and Evil as Circuit Design

*Good and Evil: A Structural Essay* argues that the old moral words survive because they name a real structural split: thought that makes power visible versus thought that gives power narrative cover. The conservation model makes that split almost diagrammable.

Evil, in this register, is a *blame pump*. [definition:C43] It is machinery for moving blame *against* its natural gradient of causation, away from those whose decisions produced the harm and toward those least able to refuse it. The ancient scapegoat ritual, in which a community's sins were laid on an animal and driven into the wilderness, is the pump in its purest form. [interpretation:C44] Its modern versions are quieter: the poor blamed for poverty, the user blamed for the design, the subordinate blamed for the order. In every case the pump needs the same fuel: *opacity*. Blame can only be routed against causation where causation cannot be seen. [hypothesis:C45]

Good, correspondingly, is *circuit visibility*. [definition:C46] It is the insistence that the wiring be drawn, so blame can be matched to structure rather than to impedance. That is why the structural essay finds that authority is hypersensitive to being seen. A visible circuit cannot run a pump. [model:C47]

And *hypocrisy*, in Section VI of that essay's sense, is a ledger kept off the books: a public accounting in which blame flows one way, and a private accounting, inside the inner circle, in which it flows another. [model:C48]

None of this requires angels or devils. It requires only an ammeter, and the willingness to look at it.

## X. Where the Model Breaks

A model that cannot fail is not a model. Here, honestly, are its leaks.

*Blame can be created from nothing.* Moral panics manufacture enormous quantities of blame without any corresponding harm. [interpretation:C49] A rumor can charge an entire population. So the system is not closed; there are sources. The First Law holds best for blame that *originates* in a real harm, which makes "where did this blame come from?" one of the most useful questions anyone can ask. [hypothesis:C50]

*Blame is not a single substance.* Legal liability, moral fault, and social disgrace move along different paths at different speeds. [interpretation:C51] A person can be legally cleared and socially destroyed. The model flattens these into one quantity for clarity, and the clarity has a price.

And there is one operation that genuinely destroys blame.

It is not punishment. Punishment converts blame into heat, and the total is visibly preserved, which is why punishment so often breeds new grievances, vendettas, and cycles. [hypothesis:C52] It is not time either, since time only diffuses blame. It is not settlement, which only converts blame into money.

The operation is *forgiveness*.

Forgiveness is the one non-conservative force in the moral universe. [model:C53] When an injured party truly forgives, blame does not move somewhere else. It leaves the system. No one receives it, no sink absorbs it, and no ledger records a transfer. It is the only accounting operation that cannot be audited, because nothing is left over to audit.

That is why nearly every enduring religious tradition placed forgiveness near its center, and why nearly every bureaucracy treats it with suspicion. [conjecture:C54] An institution can transfer, escrow, convert, and diffuse blame. *An institution cannot forgive.* [interpretation:C55] Only the injured can, and the authority to destroy blame is the one authority that cannot be delegated upward. A system that depends on routing blame has every reason to fear the one power it cannot route.

This is also why forgiveness cannot be demanded. A forgiveness extracted under pressure is not destruction; it is one more transfer, with the blame quietly moving from the offender onto the victim who "failed to let it go." Forced forgiveness is a pump wearing the clothes of grace. [interpretation:C56]

## Conclusion

### The Man Who Refused to Be Dissipated

In 399 BCE, Athens needed somewhere to put its blame. The city had lost a war, survived an oligarchic terror, and restored a fragile democracy. Every account was in deficit. It found a low-impedance terminal: an old man, poor, ugly, famously annoying, with no faction to defend him. [interpretation:C57]

The engine ran as designed. The blame was concentrated into a single accountable unit and converted into heat in the form of a cup of hemlock. [interpretation:C58]

But Socrates did something the model does not expect a sink to do. He refused every route of escape. He would not flee, though his friends had arranged it, and he would not plead, though pleading might have worked. He would not let the charge diffuse into exile or fog. He held the blame in full view, at full concentration, until it killed him. In doing so he turned it into the one thing a blame engine cannot easily dispose of: *a permanent public record of where the charge had been sent.* [interpretation:C59]

Twenty-four centuries later the circuit is still visible, and every student who reads the *Apology* traces the wiring again and asks who was really blamed, for what, and why.

This is not a recommendation to drink poison. It is an observation about design. A sink that refuses to dissipate becomes a mirror. Blame that cannot be hidden becomes information, among the most efficient conversions any engine has run. [hypothesis:C60]

So, a closing exercise in the old manner. Pick any institution you belong to: a school, a company, a family, a state. Ask four questions:

1. When something goes wrong here, *where does the blame go?*
2. Is that where the *causes* are, or where the *impedance* is lowest?
3. What fraction of the blame becomes *information*, and what fraction becomes *heat*?
4. Who here has the power to *forgive*, and who is only allowed to *forward*?

Then, in fairness, ask what would show your answers wrong, and go look.

The laws of thermodynamics did not make engines moral. They made engines *understandable*, and understandable engines could be redesigned. Blame is the oldest fuel in human society. [guess:C61] We have burned it for as long as there have been human societies, and we have rarely measured the efficiency. It is time to start. [normative:C62]

## Limits

The model is an analogy without units. Blame has no agreed measure, so the conservation law cannot be tested as stated, and the quantities named here (impedance, learning efficiency) are metaphors until someone operationalizes them. Section X lists the leaks the essay already knows about: blame can be manufactured, it is not a single substance, and forgiveness removes it without a trace.

The empirical anchors are thin. Commercial aviation's safety record has many causes besides its reporting culture. The moral crumple zone rests on a small set of case studies and has not been shown to hold across all automated systems. The claim that punishment competes with learning has support in aviation and medicine, and the essay has not shown that it holds for courts, schools, or families. Table 1 is the author's impression, not data.

Rival accounts exist. René Girard's theory of the scapegoat mechanism treats scapegoating as a way of discharging social tension, not as the routing of a conserved quantity. Historians disagree about why Athens tried Socrates: some stress his associations with Critias and Alcibiades, others stress the religious charges, and the claim that he had no one to defend him is an interpretation, not a settled fact. The prediction about AI liability may fail simply because the conflicts turn out to be about capability or consciousness after all.

Several citations remain to be supplied for the aviation reporting claims, and the references to *The Ego Manifesto*, *Good and Evil: A Structural Essay*, and *The New Adventures of Socrates* are by title only until their slugs are confirmed.
