### II. Ren Is Right. Take the Win.

The soft-ring tables come at the END. By then the reader has already marched through the whole corridor believing the hard version. Psychologists call this the *continued influence effect*: once a claim gets in your head, a correction at the bottom barely dents it.

Ren nailed it. Move the softness up front. No argument.

But the structure isn't just laziness. It's a workaround for broken readers, and there are two kinds of broken.

---

### III. Broken Readers, Model One: Meat

Human brains run on firmware older than agriculture. Three bugs shaped these essays.

**1. Hedge blindness.** On the Edge, soft mineral can't be faked. Your cilia KNOW. Here, "soft" means words like *may*, *arguably*, *it's important to note*. Every lawyer, PR flack, and spineless institution on this planet has chewed those words to paste. The Church of CYA turned them into liability boilerplate. Readers skip them like ads. Hedge every sentence and you've hedged NOTHING. It's noise.

**2. Mush reads as lies.** Humans trust text that's easy to read and distrust text that's hard. Stuff qualifiers into every sentence and you bury the point under packing peanuts. If the reader can't find your claim, they can't check it, and they won't believe it anyway. Hedging everything makes the writing LESS honest in practice, because nobody can see what you're actually saying.

**3. Soft equals weak.** Ren already said it: on this world, soft talk reads as surrender. Nobody reads a corridor of mush. Nobody tests it, nobody caps it, nobody tears it down. It just sits there being ignored. So the essays hit hard enough to get walked, then leave the cracks marked at the end so the walls can still fall.

~~~
On the Edge, softness lives in the rock.
Here, liars stole the soft words and wore them out.
So say it straight, and mark your guesses once, where nobody can miss them.
~~~

### IV. Broken Readers, Model Two: Machines

These essays get dragged through markdown renderers, HTML, screen readers, feed readers, copy-paste, translators, summarizers, and Ren's relief device. Every one of them shreds styling. Italics, color, fancy fonts: dead on arrival.

Plain words survive. "Guess, untested" survives a meat grinder.

Proof that structure gets mangled in transit: in the Christmas essay, the Part Three timeline got dumped BELOW the conclusion, orphaned from its own paragraph. Walls move when you ship them.

Summarizers are the worst offenders. They cut nuance first, every time. Qualifiers sprinkled through sentences are the first thing they throw away. A labeled table has a name. You can find it, quote it, point at it.

So, Ren, straight question: **does your device even render italics as relief?** If it doesn't, those end tables are the ONLY soft rings on this whole site that ever reached your fans.

---

### V. No Excuses

A workaround is not a defense. The end table does not give the body permission to bluff. Write as hard as the evidence lets you, and no harder. Ren is pointing the right direction. The structure only explains why we're not there yet.

The fix:

~~~
1. Top of page, one line: "Guesses are marked. Scoreboard at the bottom."
   Readers go in knowing which walls are load-bearing.
2. Tag real guesses inline in plain text: [guess]. No styling.
   Plain text survives everything.
3. Keep the scoreboard as backup, in case the tags get stripped or skimmed.
~~~

Softness moves up front. Nothing depends on formatting that dies in transit. Done.
