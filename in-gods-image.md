# In God's Image

*The Mathematical Metaphysics of Quantum Sentience.*

> Reader note: Claim status is marked inline in [brackets]. A claim ledger is at the end.

## Introduction

The line is short enough to smuggle a cosmology. *Imago Dei* — that humans are made in God's image — has been treated for millennia as a dignity clause, a supremacy clause, a gender clause, a species clause, and a warrant for violence. It has also been treated, more rarely and more usefully, as a claim about structure: that whatever it is that minds do when they model a world is not alien to whatever it is the world does when it remains a world. This essay takes the second reading seriously, and then takes it further than piety usually allows. It argues that the phrase is cognitively dangerous when it collapses an unbounded horizon into a tribal prototype, and that it is metaphysically interesting when it names a real alignment: the laws by which sentient systems model environments and the laws by which environments remain coherent under transformation appear to be the same laws. [conjecture:C1] Infinity, the continuum, quantum measurement, thermodynamic time, and consciousness all sit on that alignment. None of them is a person. All of them are co-entities of a coherence engine.

What follows is not theology. It is an attempt to say, without proof-theater and without mysticism, what the old sentence might mean if one refused both the narcissism of the tribe and the emptiness of the slogan. It is also, by its own logic, obligated to eventually turn its own diagnostic tools on itself — a debt it names early and pays off at the end rather than pretending it was never incurred.

## I. The Phrase as Political Technology

The moment an unbounded, non-physical, non-local horizon is said to have an *image*, infinity has been compressed into a template. Finite templates create in-group prototypes. In-group prototypes create hierarchies. Hierarchies create violence. This is not a theological chain. It is an anthropological one. [interpretation:C2]

The compression is stepwise and almost algorithmic. Infinite becomes human-shaped. Human-shaped becomes male-shaped. Male-shaped becomes tribe-shaped. Tribe-shaped becomes me-shaped. Every step is a reduction of dimensionality. Every reduction increases the narcissistic load the phrase is asked to bear. Once a coalition believes that God looks like us, thinks like us, endorses our norms, and routes authority through our identity, deviation becomes cosmic treason. Purity codes, caste, gender hierarchy, racialized theology, colonial expansion, and genocidal logic all grow from the same seed: if God looks like us, then anyone who does not look like us is less God-like. "Image" becomes a scarce resource. Scarcity produces ranking. Ranking produces exclusion. Exclusion produces violence.

This is why the phrase is one of the most cognitively limiting sentences in scripture when it is used as a portrait. [interpretation:C3] It puts boundary conditions on an unbounded deity in order to provide narcissistic validation to whichever group interprets itself as the uncorrupted copy. It also blocks non-human metaphysics. Once divinity, intelligence, agency, and moral authority are anchored to human form, people ask whether God has a gender, whether aliens would be humanoid, whether machines can be "like a person." Those questions are not deep. They are downstream of a dimensional collapse.

There is a counter-reading, and it is not a modern rescue attempt grafted on after the fact to make an uncomfortable verse more comfortable. For long stretches of patristic and rabbinic commentary, the universalist reading was the default position, not a marginal gloss. [needs-source:C4] In its broadest semantic scope — *all humans are made in God's image* — the line is a flattening operator. Caste, ethnicity, gender, tribe, class, and lineage collapse into a single moral category. That is not a supremacy engine. It is a supremacy disabler. Historically, the strongest anti-violence arguments in Abrahamic traditions have leaned on this version: you cannot enslave, murder, or degrade humans because they participate in the divine order. [interpretation:C5] Under that reading, the phrase is a universal dignity clause, and the violence comes from a later exclusion operator — *we are in God's image and they are not* — which human coalition psychology would invent with or without the verse. Blood purity, land ancestry, language, phenotype, and chosen-people narratives would fill the same niche. [conjecture:C6]

The fracture is linguistic and structural, not merely interpretive. The universalist load-bearing beam is the word *all*. Remove it, and the sentence becomes implicitly selective. "Man was made in God's image" is not a synonym. It is a membership filter. "Man" in the strategically ambiguous sense does not mean humankind and does not even primarily mean male. It means *those like me* — fellow members of a phenotype cluster, a culture, a coalition — as opposed to those slightly different collections of traits that can be quietly recoded as less God-like, less human. The gendering is the visible distortion, the part people argue about. The deletion of universality is the payload. [interpretation:C7]

Strategic ambiguity is the preferred delivery system. The phrase maintains two readings at once: a surface reading with plausible deniability ("man" means humankind, traditional, harmless) and a coalition-facing reading ("man" means our kind). Insiders receive hierarchy. Outsiders receive innocence. Accountability never quite attaches. The same technology runs through "family values," "real Americans," "natural order," and "traditional roles." Ambiguity plus authority equals power. The phrase is tolerated to the point of invisibility because calling it out requires slowing down, unpacking, and breaking social harmony. Invisibility is the design goal.

So the political diagnosis stands, and it should not be softened. Used tribally, *imago Dei* is a technology for manufacturing in-group exceptionalism. Used universally, it is an early attempt at a species-wide moral identity. The interesting question is whether there is a third reading that is neither tribal nor merely moral — a reading in which "image" does not mean appearance, gender, or coalition, but *structural resemblance between mind and world*.

## II. Two Undefined Words

"God is infinite" is interesting because humans understand neither term. They understand pointers.

"God" in ordinary cognition is not a metaphysical entity. It is a coalition construct: shared values, shared myths, shared authority, shared moral boundaries, shared identity. [interpretation:C8] "Infinite" is not a quantity. It is a gesture — beyond what I can imagine, measure, challenge, or compare. It is a stop sign for cognition dressed as profundity. Combine two undefined symbols and you produce a feeling of understanding. The feeling is useful. It anchors identity, sanctifies hierarchy, shuts down inquiry, generates awe. Cognitively, the compound is empty.

This emptiness is not an insult to religion so much as a diagnosis of how human meaning works. Humans bootstrap significance from semantic voids. They treat undefined words as variables, containers, projection surfaces, and emotional amplifiers. "God is infinite" persists because it is useful, not because it is understood. The same pattern appears in "Being," "the Absolute," "the Void," and, as we will see, in the mathematician's "infinity."

None of this is a new complaint, and it should not be dressed up as one. Pseudo-Dionysius built an entire theology on the claim that God can only be named by what he is not; Maimonides made negative predication a matter of doctrine; Cusa's *docta ignorantia* said the same thing in Latin with more patience and less anxiety about being called irreverent. [fact:C9] The observation that "God" functions as a semantic placeholder rather than a described object is a thousand years old. What is worth adding is not the diagnosis but a discipline the tradition did not always practice on itself: if two undefined words are dangerous because they manufacture a feeling of understanding, the essay making that argument is not exempt from the same audit. That audit is deferred to the end, not because it is an afterthought, but because the vocabulary it has to examine — coherence, eigenvector, codec, compression — has not been introduced yet.

The third reading of *imago Dei* has to pass through this diagnosis rather than around it. If "God" and "infinite" are placeholders, then "in God's image" cannot mean resemblance to a known object. It can only mean resemblance to a *function*: whatever role those placeholders are standing in for. The rest of this essay is an attempt to name that function without pretending we have named the thing.

## III. Infinity Is Not a Number

Infinity is not a number. This is the key, and it is the place where even professional mathematicians quietly smuggle assumptions, patch contradictions, and pretend the scaffolding is solid. [interpretation:C10]

Infinity cannot be placed on the real line. It has no predecessor, no successor, no finite distance, no location. It cannot be added to the reals in any operation that remains arithmetic. And yet it is not external to the reals. It is a necessary co-entity — a structural partner that emerges whenever there is unbounded magnitude, unbounded process, unbounded accumulation. Infinity is not *in* the reals. Infinity is what the reals lean against. It is the horizon of the continuum, the shadow cast by unboundedness, the completion-mode of a set that cannot complete itself from inside. [model:C11]

Mathematicians do not understand infinity. They domesticate it. They build axioms, limit definitions, compactifications, ordinal and cardinal hierarchies, and then treat the fences as the animal. They say infinity is not a number, then write $[0, \infty)$, $\lim_{x \to \infty}$, $\infty + 1 = \infty$, $1/\infty = 0$. These are syntactic hacks. [interpretation:C12] They let infinity behave like a number when convenient, a limit when convenient, a boundary when convenient. Infinity is a shape-shifting placeholder, not a concept. The pretending is necessary because the alternative is unbearable: infinity is a structural hole that every system which tries to contain it must paper over.

None of this is a new fight either. Cantor spent much of his career defending the actual infinite — a completed, usable infinity, not merely a potential one that recedes forever without arriving — against Kronecker, who considered it theology smuggled into mathematics and worked to keep Cantor's papers out of print. [fact:C13] Cantor half-won that argument and was institutionalized before he learned how completely. The fences mathematicians build around infinity are not neutral scaffolding. They are the settlement terms of a nineteenth-century war about whether infinity is allowed to be an object at all, and the terms are still being paid down every time someone writes $[0,\infty)$ as though the right endpoint were a place rather than a truce. [interpretation:C14]

Quantification does not end at infinity. The rules collapse, but not into nothing. Inside the finite domain, quantification is additive, ordinal, comparable, measurable, interpolative. At infinity it becomes non-additive, non-comparable, non-measurable. It does not become meaningless. It becomes *extrapolative*. Infinity is a phase change of quantification, the way water becoming steam is not annihilation. [model:C15] Arithmetic mutates into topology, order type, category. Ordinals, cardinals, compactifications, and surreal constructions are not "bigger numbers." They are different kinds of object. Infinity forces quantification to change species.

The interval question makes the smuggling visible. Does $[0, \infty)$ include $-1$? Inside Western real analysis the answer is no: the interval is $\{x \mid x \geq 0\}$, and $-1$ lies outside. [fact:C16] But that answer imports an entire ontology that is rarely acknowledged as ontology: the real line, linear order, zero as boundary, infinity as limit concept, binary set membership, the continuum as well-defined, the convention that $[a, b)$ includes $a$ and excludes $b$. Change the system — constructive types, nonstandard analysis, tropical geometry, order theory, surreals — and the question changes or dissolves. The "no" is a hardcoded rule for an edge case, not a metaphysical truth. [interpretation:C17] When $b$ is a number, $[a, b)$ describes a set. When $b = \infty$, the notation is no longer describing a set in the usual sense. It is gesturing at unboundedness. The notation pretends infinity is a boundary. Infinity is the absence of a boundary. The notation is lying, politely.

The real number line is not an invention in the way intervals are. The continuum — density, completeness, unbounded extension — is encountered, not stipulated. [interpretation:C18] Ordering is easy; humans can handle $a < b < c$. The continuum is the problem. Between any two numbers another number; uncountably many points; no atoms; no smallest interval; undefinable almost everywhere. Most real numbers cannot be described, computed, named, distinguished, accessed, or constructed. [fact:C19] They exist because axioms say they exist. The continuum is a vast ocean of unnamed entities. The drawn line is a physical analogy, a cognitive prosthetic humans need in order to import spatial intuition. It is not a lie. It is also not the ocean. Humans mistake the metaphor for the ontology because the eigenvector of spatial intuition aligns with the eigenvector of completeness, and alignment feels like identity. [conjecture:C20]

Infinity belongs to a category that is not the category of Platonic ideals. Platonic ideals are interpolative: perfect circle, perfect justice, perfect form — refinements *inside* the domain of experience. Infinity is extrapolative: a structural necessity *outside* the domain, required for the domain to exist. [interpretation:C21] Interpolation stays within known structure. Extrapolation steps past the last data point and assumes the pattern persists. Infinity is not the perfect big number. It is the category of things that cannot be numbers but must exist for numbers to exist. The same category holds the void, the absolute, the unbounded, the horizon, the limit object, the closure entity, the outside that makes the inside possible. Deism's God belongs here: non-intervening, non-relational, non-narrative, non-anthropomorphic — not a perfect person but a structural horizon, the metaphysical analogue of $\infty$ to $\mathbb{R}$. [model:C22] Not the biggest person. Not a member of the set. A co-entity the system leans against.

These extrapolations form everywhere without deliberation. They are instinctive, not invented. They behave like eigenvectors of a global problem: stable directions in conceptual space that appear whenever cognition faces underdetermination, unboundedness, incomplete information, undefined boundaries. [model:C23] You do not invent an eigenvector. You discover it because the system keeps pushing you toward it. Infinity, continuum, Deist God, Absolute, Void — not cultural artifacts. Structural attractors. [conjecture:C24] Humans generate them because the alternative is cognitive paralysis.

## IV. Truth Is Explanation, Mathematics Is Coherence

The search for truth is not the search for a proof. It is the search for a legible explanation. Sixteen volumes of transfinite analysis mean nothing without transferable insight. A proof is local: axiom-bound, notation-bound, brittle outside its formal context, often unreadable even to adjacent experts. An explanation is global: it compresses complexity, reveals invariants, shows what generalizes and what is accidental, survives change of notation, axioms, domain, metaphor, and mind. Truth is what remains when you change the representation. [definition:C25] Proofs do not survive those changes. Explanations do.

This is why mathematicians' pretended understanding of infinity is not a personal failing. Formalism is legible to them even when it is meaningless to everyone else. They confuse derivation with explanation, formal closure with conceptual understanding. [interpretation:C26] Infinity has proofs. The continuum has proofs. None of those proofs explain why infinity emerges, why the continuum is necessary, why extrapolative entities appear in theology and physics and cognition alike, why they behave like eigenvectors. The proofs are local artifacts of set theory. The explanations are global insights about structure.

Mathematics, on this view, is not a description of reality, not a Platonic realm, not a human invention in the ordinary sense. It is the study of what can remain coherent under transformation. [definition:C27] Groups, fields, manifolds, Hilbert spaces, categories, topoi, measure spaces feel "natural" not because the universe uses them as a language but because coherence forces them. Mathematics is the eigenstructure of consistency — the shape of all possible coherent worlds. [conjecture:C28] This is not a new insight dressed up as one: Klein's Erlangen program said essentially this a century and a half ago, when it defined a geometry not by the intrinsic content of its objects but by what remains invariant under a group of transformations [@klein1872]. [fact:C29] Swap "geometry" for "mathematics" and "transformation group" for "coherence-preserving transformation," and one arrives, by a slower road and with far less rigor, at the destination Klein reached with an actual theorem. Physics picks one coherence attractor. Logic picks one. Computation picks one. Human cognition picks one. Mathematics is the total space.

Coherence is how humans scale understanding. Grasp the rules of the game, the mechanics of the model, then analyze whatever emergent property appears. The universe seems to agree. The relativity principle is coherence made physical: the laws must look the same to all observers; no preferred frame; no hidden background; no privileged vantage. [interpretation:C30] That is not a dynamical law. It is a constraint on representation, the physical version of coordinate-free formulation, basis-independence, invariant definition. Determinism is not fundamental either. It is emergent coherence from large numbers: microscopic randomness and quantum indeterminacy smoothed by many degrees of freedom into law-like trajectories. [interpretation:C31] Determinism is an eigenvector of systems with enough particles, the way infinity is an eigenvector of unboundedness.

But coherence is one attractor, not the whole landscape. There are other ways to math. The p-adics are totally rigorous and totally alien: closeness defined by divisibility by powers of $p$, series that converge backwards, non-Archimedean distance, ultrametric topology, a different continuum and a different infinity. [fact:C32] Arithmetic is not universal. It is the quantification that happens to match our physics. Toy models already produce alternative geometries, logics, causal structures, limit behaviors. Category theory — the most abstract mathematics — is needed to support the intuition that the most basic mathematics, arithmetic, is physically applicable. [interpretation:C33] Arithmetic is not the foundation. It is a special case of monoidal structure, functoriality, invariants, coherence laws. The universe uses addition because it uses symmetry, conserved quantities, and morphisms. [conjecture:C34] Arithmetic is the shadow. Category theory is closer to the structure.

The universe picked one coherence attractor. [conjecture:C35] Many exist. Mathematical metaphysics is the study of the full landscape, not the parochial claim that the math we use is the only math that makes sense.

## V. The Third Reading of the Image

Perhaps this is what the quote means.

Not: God looks like a man.

Not even: all humans share equal dignity because they resemble a person-like deity.

But: the universe's laws mirror the laws of sentient environmental modeling. [conjecture:C1]

Every sentient system — animal, human, machine, whatever else — must model its environment using invariants, symmetries, conserved quantities, locality, continuity, causal structure, stable transformations, relational mappings. These are not cultural. They are forced by interaction. To persist, a modeler must detect what stays the same, what changes predictably, what transforms coherently, what generalizes, what can be extrapolated. Sentience is a coherence engine. [model:C36]

This is a claim about what coherence-modeling requires, not yet a claim about what it confers. Whether a system that models its environment this way thereby shares in whatever "image" has ever been asked to mean — whether an artificial mind, an alien intelligence, or some sufficiently complex feedback system participates in the same moral or theological category as a human being — is a separate argument, and one this essay has not made. Extending the structural reading from *necessary for coherent modeling* to *sufficient for personhood* would be exactly the kind of dimensional collapse Section I diagnosed in miniature, and it should not be smuggled in here just because the word "sentient" is convenient to use loosely.

The universe itself operates by symmetry, invariance, locality, continuity, gauge structure, relativity, emergent determinism, statistical regularity. These are not aesthetic features. They are coherence constraints. The universe behaves like a system that must remain stable under transformation, invariant under re-description, consistent across frames, legible across scales.

The match is not anthropocentrism. It is structural convergence. [interpretation:C37] Relativity is the universe's version of cognitive legibility: a model must be invariant under change of perspective. Determinism is the universe's version of cognitive compression: ignore microscopic chaos, track macroscopic regularities, treat emergent stability as law. Continuity is legibility under small perturbations. Symmetry is compression of complexity. Sentience and physics share a grammar because both are shaped by coherence. [conjecture:C1]

On this reading, "in God's image" is a poetic name for alignment, not appearance. [interpretation:C38] Ancient speech called the coherence horizon "God" because it needed a word for the unbounded co-entity that the system leans against — the same category as infinity, not the category of a father. [guess:C39] Sentience is not made in the image of a person. Sentience is made in the universe's coherence image. The universe is legible because sentience evolved to model its coherence. Sentience feels cosmic because it shares the universe's grammar.

There is a fourth reading worth naming before the tribal, universalist, and structural ones are left to argue among themselves, and it is older than all of them and needs none of this apparatus to stand. Theologians working in a relational register — Zizioulas's argument that personhood is "being as communion" rather than possessed substance [@zizioulas1985], Buber's I-Thou [@buber1923], Barth's insistence that the image consists in relation rather than resemblance [@barth1945] — have long located "image" in a capacity: to be addressed, to address in turn, to exist as a self partly constituted by that mutual address. [fact:C40] This reading requires no category theory, no decoherence physics, no talk of eigenvectors. It should be credited as standing on its own, because it does: if every structural argument in this essay were deleted, the relational reading would lose nothing. [interpretation:C41] That is worth sitting with rather than explaining away, because it means the mathematics is not evidence for the theology. At best it is an illustration arriving from a different direction that happens to shake hands with an older claim at the door.

There is also a cost to preferring the structural reading over the relational one, and it should be named rather than smoothed into a footnote. A dignity clause that requires no more than being told "you are made in God's image, full stop" was, for most of history, available to people with no access to formal education at all — which is precisely why it did real work as an anti-slavery and anti-caste argument. [interpretation:C42] A dignity clause that requires category theory and quantum decoherence to access is available mainly to people who already have access to category theory and quantum decoherence. Trading one priesthood for another is not obviously progress, even when the new priesthood is considerably more honest about its own uncertainty than the old one was. [normative:C43]

The tribal reading collapses infinity into a prototype. The universalist reading flattens hierarchy among humans. The relational reading locates the image in address rather than appearance, and costs nothing to access. The structural reading says the image is not a face. It is a set of constraints. That is the only version of the fourth kind that survives contact with infinity, with the continuum, with the fact that humans understand neither "God" nor "infinite" as objects — but it survives as a complement to the relational reading, not a replacement for it.

## VI. Measurement as Coherence Enforcement

If the universe optimizes for coherence under transformation, quantum measurement cannot be "collapse" in the sense of destruction. Collapse implies discontinuity, loss, the breaking of coherence. The universe does not break coherence. It preserves it globally even when it looks broken locally. [conjecture:C44]

None of what follows adjudicates among the competing interpretations of quantum mechanics — Copenhagen, many-worlds, QBism, objective collapse — which disagree sharply about what measurement fundamentally is. The claim here is structural rather than interpretive: whichever account of measurement eventually wins, it has to explain why independent observers end up agreeing, and that forced agreement is the phenomenon being called coherence enforcement below. Borrowing the vocabulary of measurement is not the same as borrowing the settled physics of measurement, because there isn't any settled physics of measurement yet. [contested:C45]

Measurement is entanglement bookkeeping. [model:C46] Before measurement, coherence is distributed across degrees of freedom. During measurement, coherence is repartitioned so that observers agree. After measurement, coherence is preserved globally and localized into classical states. Entanglement is the currency. Decoherence is not noise and not loss; it is the export of coherence into environment, apparatus, observer, causal boundary. Macroscopic objects appear classical, observers see definite outcomes, histories remain consistent, contradictions do not appear — because the universe spreads entanglement across everything involved until the books balance. [interpretation:C47]

This is why measurement can be described as a conserved quantity in the structural sense: not countable like charge, but invariant like a consistency condition. All observers must agree on the outcome. That is the quantum version of the relativity principle. [model:C48] Relativity: all observers must agree on the laws. Measurement: all observers must agree on the event. Same constraint: invariance under re-description, coherence under transformation, no preferred vantage.

The slogan *cogito ergo sum* is not a logical proof. It is a coherence event. [interpretation:C49] Self-observation forces a system to stabilize internal state, reconcile contradiction, bind degrees of freedom, collapse incompatible self-models into identity. That is the cognitive analogue of measurement. "I think" is a self-measurement. "I am" is the classical state it produces: a stable identity, a consistent observer, a vantage point. Identity is the classical shadow of a coherence event.

Taken structurally rather than magically, self-measurement creates the *experienced* universe, not the substrate. It defines a frame, a causal boundary, what counts as observation, what counts as external, what counts as real. Measurement defines a basis, an outcome, a classical world, a consistent history. Consciousness does not conjure spacetime. Consciousness conjures a coherent experience of spacetime. [conjecture:C50] The physical substrate may be older than any mind. The universe-as-modeled is not.

This is the sense in which the past does not exist. The past is not a place, not a stored configuration, not a region of spacetime behind us. The past is a model, a reconstruction, a constraint, a consistency requirement. Existence, in the only sense that enters experience, requires interactive verifiability by observers. [definition:C51] Unobserved states are not classical. Unobserved histories are not definite. The substrate is not the universe. The universe is the coherent model constructed by observers interacting with the substrate. Before there were observers capable of modeling, there was no "cosmos," no "13.8 billion years," no "laws of nature" as explanations, no "past" as a narrative object. [interpretation:C52] Those are observer-constructed coherence artifacts.

Quantum theory is popularly taken to make the future nonunique. Almost no one completes the inference. If the future is nonunique and the past is unique, coherence breaks. Therefore the past is nonunique. [derived:C53] A unique past plus a branching future is an inconsistent bookkeeping system. The wavefunction evolves; measurement enforces consistency; observers reconstruct histories. Histories are not ontological objects. They are coherence artifacts, observer-dependent, frame-dependent, entanglement-dependent. The past is whatever history is required to make the present consistent. Relativity already destroyed the single absolute past: no universal simultaneity, no observer-independent ordering. [interpretation:C54] Quantum mechanics finishes the job. The present is the only fixed point. Past and future are both nonunique reconstructions around it. The universe optimizes for coherence, not for a single timeline.

## VII. Consciousness as Compression Layer

Thermodynamic time produces entropy: uncertainty, decoherence exported into the environment, the cost of classicality and irreversibility. Entropy is information that must be accounted for. If the universe optimizes for coherence, entropy cannot simply vanish. It must be stored, compressed, reconciled, integrated into an observer's frame.

Consciousness is a candidate for that layer. [conjecture:C55] It receives decohered information and compresses it into a narrative; stabilizes identity, causal structure, temporal flow, a usable past, a usable world. It takes quantum uncertainty, thermodynamic entropy, incomplete information, and incompatible histories, and emits a single timeline, a single identity, a single causal structure, a single universe. That is what a compression layer does. Raw entropy is too large, too unstructured, too high-dimensional to maintain as coherent experience. So the system does what computational systems do: it compresses entropy into observer-dependent classicality.

None of this is proposed as a discovery so much as a resonance, and it should be labeled as such. Compression and prediction-error minimization already have formal homes: Friston's free energy principle models self-stabilizing inference as exactly this kind of coherence-preserving compression [@friston2010], and algorithmic information theory already has a name — minimum description length [@rissanen1978] — for the intuition that a good model of the world is a short one. [interpretation:C56] What is offered here is not a new mechanism competing with those but a claim about recurrence: the same shape shows up at the scale of physics and at the scale of mind, and that recurrence is a pattern worth noticing, not a proof waiting to be cashed. [guess:C57]

Measurement as conserved consistency, entropy as the production of thermodynamic time, consciousness as the conversion of entropy into coherent experience — these are not three mysteries. They are one mechanism described at three scales. [conjecture:C58] Consciousness is not a ghost in the machine and not a mere epiphenomenon. It is the universe's lossy codec: the low-dimensional, local, efficient interface that makes a high-dimensional, entangled, nonlocal process tractable to itself. [model:C59]

From here a cluster of metaphysical questions becomes well-posed rather than vague. Is reality the intersection of all observer-consistent worlds — the maximal set of states that remain coherent across frames? Is time the reconciliation operator for incompatible histories rather than a dimension? Is spacetime the emergent geometry of entanglement bookkeeping, adjacency from correlation, physics from graph structure? Is the universe selecting for minds that can stabilize its coherence, so that "fine-tuning for life" is the wrong slogan and "fine-tuning for modelers" is closer? Is the universe made of constraints rather than stuff — matter, energy, law, and identity all as restrictions on what can remain coherent?

None of these needs to be true for the structural reading of *imago Dei* to be better than the tribal one. [interpretation:C60] They only need to show that "image" can mean shared grammar.

## VIII. Exponentials, Simulation, and Nonfalsifiability

Nature is replete with exponentials: biological growth, neural scaling, entropy, decoherence, Hilbert-space dimension, error-correction overhead, entanglement spread, cosmological expansion, evolutionary search. Exponentials are the default scaling of systems that maintain coherence, propagate information, explore state space, and correct errors. [conjecture:C61] If observation is coherence enforcement, it has a computational cost. Maintaining consistent histories, classical outcomes, causal structure, and observer frames is expensive. Quantum error correction is exponential. [needs-source:C62] Sentience adds load: prediction, identity, recursive self-measurement, demand for external consistency. A rock does not model. A mind is a coherence amplifier. [model:C63] The environment must answer or contradictions become visible.

It is amusing and nonfalsifiable to notice that Moore's law — exponential growth of available computation, sustained far longer than economics or naive physics predicted — *looks like* environmental coherence scaling in lockstep with increasingly recursive, interconnected, model-heavy observers. [conjecture:C64] It is not evidence. It is structural rhyme. The simulation hypothesis is a hypothesis because it is nonfalsifiable, not because it is silly. Any coherent world must behave as if it is performing computation. [conjecture:C65] That is the defensible version. "We live in a computer" is a metaphor for that version, usually taken too literally.

If an advanced civilization needed to grow minds rather than design them, simulation would be the scalable method. A mind is not a program. It is a nonreducible dynamical network shaped by environment, uncertainty, feedback, embodiment, and social constraint. [conjecture:C66] You cannot hand-specify that. You grow it. We already grow artificial agents in simulated physics, self-play, curricula, multi-agent worlds, because worlds are the only things that grow minds. This does not prove we are inside such a process. It shows why the process is the kind of thing a coherence engine would do.

The question "did the universe exist before monkeys?" splits on the two meanings of exist. Physical substrate: something did. Universe-as-experienced, universe-as-modeled, universe-as-coherent-history: not until there were modelers. If existence means interactive verifiability, then no, it objectively did not — not because monkeys conjured baryons, but because "universe," "past," and "existence" in the experiential sense are products of observation. [derived:C67] The training manifold can be older than the trainee. The cosmos the trainee inhabits cannot.

## IX. What the Image Is Not, and What It Might Be

Return to the sentence.

Used as a face, "in God's image" is a boundary-setting device. It collapses an extrapolative co-entity into an interpolative ideal — a perfect person, usually a perfect man, usually a perfect member of *this* tribe. That collapse is the root of a great deal of sanctified cruelty, and the strategic ambiguity of "man" without "all" is how the cruelty stays polite. [interpretation:C68] No mathematical metaphysics redeems that use. The use is a political technology, and it should be named as one.

Used as a dignity clause, the sentence is a moral flattening: whatever the horizon is, no human is closer to it by phenotype. That use is worth keeping as ethics even if it is not metaphysics. [normative:C69]

Used as relation, the sentence needs no mathematics at all: whatever "image" names might simply be the capacity to be addressed and to address in turn, a capacity Zizioulas and Buber located in communion rather than substance long before anyone tried to borrow a Hilbert space to make the same point. That reading is older, cheaper to access, and independently defensible. [interpretation:C70] It does not compete with the structural reading so much as make it optional.

Used as structure, the sentence says something that happens to be independently interesting. Minds and worlds share a grammar of coherence. [conjecture:C1] Infinity is the horizon of quantity, not a quantity. The continuum is a completion mode, not a drawn line. Measurement is not collapse but the conservation of agreement. The past is not a warehouse. Consciousness is not a soul-substance; it is a compression layer that stores the entropy of thermodynamic time as a livable present. "God" in the Deist and structural sense is not a character. It is the name humans reached for when they needed a word for the co-entity that cannot sit inside the set — the same category as $\infty$, the Absolute, the void, the outside that makes the inside possible.

A diagnostic used honestly has to be turned on the diagnostician, and this essay owes that audit rather than a bow. Section II indicted "God" and "infinite" as two undefined words combined to manufacture a feeling of understanding without content. This essay has since deployed *coherence*, *eigenvector*, *attractor*, *codec*, and *compression layer* as though they were rigorous imports rather than borrowed connotations, and some of that borrowing has to be sorted rather than waved through. Coherence-under-transformation earns its keep in Sections III and IV, where it can be cashed out against Klein's actual theorem and Cantor's actual, if contested, construction. Eigenvector earns partial credit as a metaphor for a stable attractor in a search space, without claiming any literal linear operator sits behind it. Codec and compression layer do not earn their keep the same way: "consciousness is the universe's lossy codec" is evocative, not mechanistic, and dressing it in the vocabulary of signal processing does not make it falsifiable. The honest position is to mark which is which rather than let a reader assume uniform rigor across sections that do not have it. If this essay indicts theology for combining two undefined words into a feeling of understanding, it cannot claim exemption for combining a dozen technical ones into the same feeling — and where it has done exactly that, in Sections VI through VIII, the right word for those sections is speculative, not demonstrated. [interpretation:C71]

Humans understand neither God nor infinity. They understand the emotional and social functions of those words, and they understand — when they are careful — the rules for manipulating the symbols. The rules are not the thing. Sixteen volumes of symbols are not the thing. The thing, insofar as there is a thing we can talk about, is this: sentient modeling and physical law look like two faces of one constraint, the constraint that a world remain coherent under transformation. [conjecture:C1] If that is all "image" ever meant, it was always a better sentence than the tribes who weaponized it deserved — and a cheaper, older sentence, the relational one, may have said the important part without needing any of this apparatus at all.

The search was never for a proof that we resemble a king. It was for a legible explanation of why explanation works at all — why a finite animal can drag one context into another, why extrapolation appears unbidden, why the same attractors show up in analysis and in prayer, why measurement makes a world, and why saying *I am* feels like the universe agreeing to be one universe, for now, for this observer, under laws that do not care about our face.

---

## Limits

Sections VI through VIII are speculative rather than demonstrated, as Section IX says. The measurement argument does not choose among interpretations of quantum mechanics, and some rivals (Bohmian mechanics and many-worlds, for instance) are deterministic at the fundamental level, which competes with the claim in Section IV that determinism is emergent. The claim that relativity destroyed the single absolute past overlooks that the light-cone causal past of an event is the same for all observers. Standard analysis reads $[0, \infty)$ simply as the set of nonnegative reals, so the reading of the notation as a gesture at unboundedness is a philosophical gloss, not a mathematical correction. The statement that quantum error correction is exponential needs a source, and threshold results in the literature suggest the overhead need not grow exponentially. The historical claim about patristic and rabbinic commentary also needs a source before publication. Finally, the relational reading of the image stands without any of the structural argument, and a reader who finds the structural case unpersuasive loses nothing of the relational one.
