# Fish People Becoming Gods

*Why the Molt Requires We Become Insect People First*

> Reader note: Claim status is marked inline in [brackets]. A claim ledger is at the end.

## Introduction

There is a story hiding inside evolutionary biology, cognitive science, and the daily news cycle, and it is stranger than anything in mythology: we are fish people. Not metaphorically — mechanistically. The circuitry that lets you read a friend's face, negotiate a marriage, or argue about politics on the internet is a direct, unbroken descendant of the neural machinery that first evolved so that a fish could avoid colliding with its neighbor in a school. [hypothesis:C1] We carry the ocean in our skulls.

This piece argues something more specific and more strange than "humans evolved from fish." It argues that:

1. Vertebrate cognition — from schooling fish to mind-reading humans — is a single continuous lineage built around modeling other minds, and this lineage is both our greatest gift and our deepest trap. [interpretation:C2]
2. That trap — an ancient coalition-and-conflict engine — is why humans cannot achieve unity, cannot make peace with our own species, and cannot even communicate meaningfully with the other minds we share a planet with. [hypothesis:C3]
3. Insects represent a wholly separate cognitive lineage — not "simpler," but *orthogonal* — built on decentralized, stigmergic, swarm-based coordination rather than mind-modeling. [interpretation:C4] This is why insects feel so deeply alien to us, more alien than most vertebrates, more alien in some ways than we imagine extraterrestrials to be.
4. Humanity is currently undergoing its first substrate molt — a transition as violent and disorienting as any in evolutionary history, indistinguishable from the inside from war, collapse, and revolution. [conjecture:C5]
5. For that molt to produce something god-like — a unified, planet-scale, self-aware civilization — rather than merely a faster, more efficient collapse, we must borrow the coordination logic of the insect lineage. [conjecture:C6] We must become, in function if not in form, insect people. Only then can the fish-people substrate molt into something genuinely new: a civilization that has escaped the coalition engine that has governed vertebrate life for five hundred million years.

This is the argument, expanded in full. Four terms recur throughout; Table 1 defines them in brief.

Table 1: Key terms used in this piece.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Proto-Scientific Instinct (PSI) | The innate, always-running human tendency to propose causal explanations from incomplete data |
| Coalition engine | The ancestral vertebrate mechanism that stabilizes in-group cohesion through contrast with an external rival or enemy |
| Stigmergy | Coordination mediated by the environment itself, rather than by modeling other agents' minds — the insect solution to collective action |
| Substrate molt | A wholesale replacement of a lineage's coordination architecture, as violent and disorienting from the inside as collapse or war |

## Part One: The Fish in the Machine

### The Proto-Scientific Instinct

Humans are theorizing animals. Long before we have evidence, we have hypotheses. Confronted with a coincidence, a pattern, an absence, a shadow, the human mind does not wait for data — it proposes a cause. This is not a cultural quirk or an educational deficiency; it is a cognitive module running constantly, beneath awareness, generating candidate causal structures from incomplete information.

Call this the **Proto-Scientific Instinct**, or PSI. [definition:C7] PSI is not "irrational." It is Bayesian inference under conditions of extreme prior-weighting: the brain does not wait for a clean likelihood function before proposing a model of hidden causes. It runs with whatever priors evolution, culture, and emotion have loaded into it, and it produces an output regardless of data quality. [model:C8] Bayes' actual insight — that causality can never be recovered from data alone, only inferred through priors — is something the human mind has been doing intuitively for millions of years, long before anyone wrote it down as a theorem.

This is why superstition is not a failure of rationality. It is PSI running with unconstrained priors. [interpretation:C9] Astrology, omens, lucky numbers, rituals, conspiracy theories, and — later — physics, chemistry, and medicine are all outputs of the same machine. Science did not replace the instinct that produces superstition. Science constrained its priors. Superstition is the larval stage of scientific theory, not its opposite. [interpretation:C9]

The question this raises is simple: where did this instinct come from? The answer takes us into the water.

### Schooling: The First Cooperative Causal Model

Long before there were tribes, long before there were mammals, long before there were even lungs, there were fish swimming in coordinated schools. Schooling looks simple from the outside — a shimmering, synchronized mass moving as one — but computationally it is nothing short of astonishing. Each fish must continuously:

- track the position and velocity of its neighbors,
- infer where those neighbors are about to move,
- adjust its own trajectory to maintain spacing,
- avoid collisions,
- and respond instantly to perturbations that ripple through the group.

This is not reflex. It is real-time, distributed causal inference among many independent agents — the first large-scale **Cooperative Causal Modeling** (CCM) system in the history of vertebrate life. [interpretation:C10] Schooling relies on a mechanosensory organ called the lateral line, which detects pressure gradients and water displacement, giving each fish a continuously updated model of its neighbors' behavior. [fact:C11]

This matters enormously, because schooling predates every other form of vertebrate social cognition. It predates flocking, herding, pack hunting, coalition politics, theory of mind, language, and superstition. It is the root system from which the entire tree of vertebrate social cognition grew. [hypothesis:C12]

### The Mutation That Made Us Social

When vertebrates crawled onto land, they lost the lateral line. [fact:C13] The water-pressure sensing organ that made schooling possible had no function in air. But the *computational need* that schooling served — inferring the behavior of nearby agents and adjusting one's own behavior to maintain group coherence — did not disappear. Evolution rarely discards a working algorithm; it repurposes the algorithm onto new sensory substrates.

And so the schooling circuit mutated in function. [hypothesis:C14] On land, it became:

- territorial spacing and ritualized displays in reptiles,
- flocking coordination in birds (a convergent reinvention using vision and vestibular sense rather than the lateral line),
- pack coordination and cooperative hunting in mammals,
- coalition politics and belief-tracking in primates,
- and finally, in humans, a fully generalized, cross-domain, recursive mind-modeling engine.

The question schooling answers — "where will my neighbor move next?" — became, step by step, "what will my neighbor do?", then "what does my neighbor want?", then "what does my neighbor believe?", and finally "what is my neighbor's entire model of the world?" This is the ladder from motion-prediction to theory of mind. PSI, the proto-scientific instinct, is simply this same lineage generalized to its furthest possible extent: a machine originally built to keep fish from colliding with each other, now used to build metaphysics, invent gods, write physics textbooks, and generate conspiracy theories about the moon landing.

> *We are fish people. Every handshake, every glance, every negotiated silence, every act of empathy is a schooling maneuver performed by a mind that forgot it used to live in the water.* [hypothesis:C1]

## Part Two: The Trap Inside the Gift

### The Coalition Engine

The same lineage that gave us empathy and cooperation also gave us something much darker: a coordination mechanism — the **coalition engine** — that requires an external "other" to stabilize internal cohesion. This is not a human invention. It is ancestral vertebrate architecture. [hypothesis:C15] Fish school against other schools. Wolves pack against rival packs. Primate troops maintain identity through friction with neighboring troops. The mechanism evolution converged on for maintaining group coherence without centralized control was not pure cooperation — it was cooperation stabilized by contrast, loyalty sharpened by rivalry, identity clarified by an enemy.

This coalition engine worked beautifully at the scale it evolved for: bands of a few dozen to a few hundred individuals. [estimate:C16] Humans, however, scaled far beyond that ecological range. We built cities, nations, ideologies, and planet-spanning technological systems — all while running a coordination algorithm designed for a tribe of a few hundred chimp-like primates.

We won the evolutionary game. We so thoroughly out-competed every rival and every constraint that we became, in effect, the dominant species on the planet by a staggering margin. And yet the game engine that got us here — the coalition logic requiring an enemy to hold the in-group together — never got the memo that the game was won. It cannot stop playing. Every new peace is haunted by the demand for a new "them."

This is the actual, mechanistic reason humans cannot achieve unity. [hypothesis:C3] It is not merely that we have different nations, or different religions, or different economic interests. It is that the cognitive substrate underlying all human coordination is *built* to require division. Unity, in the biological substrate, is not a hard political problem — it is a cognitive impossibility. [conjecture:C17] You cannot ask the coalition engine to produce a world without coalitions any more than you can ask a school of fish to swim without neighbors.

### The Alien Next Door

If this coalition trap were merely a human failing, it would be discouraging but survivable — the kind of flaw a species might slowly engineer around. But the deeper problem is broader still: humans, despite mastering physics and building civilizations, cannot meaningfully communicate with the other minds already sharing this planet.

Every vertebrate — fish, amphibian, reptile, bird, mammal — inherits some version of the schooling-derived mind-modeling engine. [interpretation:C2] This is why a dog's affection, a crow's grudge, a dolphin's game, and a human's love are all recognizably *the same kind of thing*, differing only in degree. Call this the universal brotherhood of vertebrates: a shared cognitive grammar built from a common ancestral circuit.

Insects sit entirely outside this brotherhood. Their lineage diverged before the schooling circuit ever evolved, [fact:C18] and they solved the problem of group coordination in a completely different way: not through modeling other minds, but through **stigmergy** — coordination mediated by the environment itself. [interpretation:C4] Pheromone trails, hardwired behavioral templates, local sensory triggers, and emergent swarm dynamics allow ant colonies and beehives to achieve extraordinary collective feats without any individual insect ever modeling the internal state of another. There is no theory of mind in an ant. [interpretation:C19] There is no belief-tracking, no intention inference, no narrative cognition. There is only a decentralized, self-organizing system responding to local gradients.

This is why insects feel more alien to us than almost any other animal on Earth — more alien, in a strange sense, than we imagine extraterrestrial life to be. [guess:C20] We can imagine an alien with intentions, a mind, a goal. We struggle to imaginatively inhabit a mind that has none of those things and yet produces breathtakingly complex collective behavior anyway.

> *A species that cannot talk to the ants under its own feet is not ready to talk to the stars.* [normative:C21]

And this is the sharpest point: if humans cannot bridge the cognitive gap between our own vertebrate lineage and the insect lineage already sharing our planet — if we cannot even build a working model of ant or bee cognition well enough to communicate with it — what hope would we have of ever meaningfully engaging an alien intelligence? Any sufficiently advanced observer would notice this immediately. [conjecture:C22] They would not judge humanity by its wars, which are a predictable and almost trivial symptom of coalition dynamics. They would judge us by our failure to unify cognitively even within our own species, and our failure to bridge cognitive architectures even with the other minds we already live beside. That failure is the real disqualifying signal. [normative:C21]

## Part Three: The Molt

### Substrate, Not Species

Evolutionary history contains a handful of moments where a lineage did not merely adapt within its existing cognitive architecture, but replaced the architecture itself. [interpretation:C23] I call such a replacement a **substrate molt**. [definition:C24] Insects performed exactly this kind of molt once already: the transition from solitary, template-driven insect cognition to eusociality. [interpretation:C25] The emergence of ants, bees, and termites represented the invention of distributed cognition, caste-based division of labor, and hive-level memory — an entirely new coordination physics layered on top of (and partially replacing) individual insect cognition.

From the inside, for the ancestral solitary insects, this transition would have been indistinguishable from catastrophe: old ecological niches destroyed, old behavioral repertoires rendered obsolete, entire lineages driven to extinction, and strange new castes rising to dominance seemingly overnight. It was not peaceful. It was not gradual in any way that would have felt survivable in the moment. It was a molt, and molts are violent by nature.

Vertebrates have never gone through an equivalent transition. We have scaled the schooling-derived coalition engine for five hundred million years without ever replacing it. Humans, right now, are the first vertebrates in history attempting exactly this kind of substrate molt — not from solitary to eusocial, but from biological, coalition-based coordination to a new, digital coordination physics. [conjecture:C5]

### Why the Digital Substrate Is Not a Vertebrate Invention

Digital systems — networks, shared databases, real-time global communication, distributed ledgers, artificial agents — do not run on coalition logic. [interpretation:C26] They do not require an external enemy to remain internally coherent. They can maintain shared state across billions of nodes without narrative cohesion, without tribal identity, without symbolic boundary-making. This is not simply "more advanced" vertebrate cognition. It is functionally closer to the insect model than to the vertebrate one: coordination achieved through shared environment and shared signal rather than through recursive modeling of individual minds. [model:C27]

This is the crucial and almost paradoxical insight: for humans to molt successfully, we do not need to become better fish. We need to become, in function, insect people. [conjecture:C6] We need our digital coordination layer to behave less like an extension of tribal identity and more like a stigmergic system — one where coordination emerges from shared, legible environmental state (open data, transparent ledgers, accessible knowledge, distributed sensing) rather than from centralized mind-modeling elites or tribal narrative warfare. [normative:C28]

The insects already solved the problem we are trying to solve: how do you coordinate an enormous number of agents without requiring each one to model the beliefs and intentions of every other one, and without requiring an enemy to hold the collective together? Their answer was stigmergy. Ours, if it succeeds, will be a digital analogue of stigmergy: systems that let humans (and non-human agents) coordinate through shared, persistent, legible signals rather than through the ancient coalition engine.

This is why the title of this piece is not a metaphor for shock value. We are fish people, running an ancient vertebrate coalition engine that cannot produce unity on its own. To transcend it, we must adopt — graft on, functionally borrow, however you want to phrase it — insect-style coordination logic through our digital substrate. [conjecture:C6] Only after that graft succeeds can what remains of "fish people" molt into something genuinely new.

### What the Molt Looks Like From Inside

Assume, for a moment, the starting conditions of a plausible near future: an escalating world war, a worsening climate crisis, dwindling stockpiles of fuel, food, and weapons, mass unemployment, and a political rupture severe enough to trigger martial law. These are not exotic assumptions; they are visible on the horizon of current trends. [interpretation:C29] Under these conditions, every legacy coordination layer — political, economic, military, ecological — begins failing simultaneously. [model:C30] Markets stop clearing. Elections stop legitimizing. Enforcement becomes patchy and local. Institutions lose operational capacity faster than they can be reformed.

This is not merely "bad." This is what a substrate invalidation event looks like from the inside. It is indistinguishable, moment to moment, from war and revolution — because it is war and revolution, viewed at the surface level. [interpretation:C31] But underneath the surface, a second process is possible: the emergence of a replacement coordination physics fast enough to catch some fraction of what the old substrate drops.

For this to be a molt rather than a mere collapse, specific things must happen. [hypothesis:C32]

- Digital coordination must persist under physical stress — mesh networks, resilient infrastructure, systems that keep routing information and decisions even when states and grids are intermittent or hostile.
- Dense, practical knowledge — engineering, medicine, agriculture, logistics, governance patterns — must survive in accessible, not merely archived, form.
- Hybrid nodes must emerge: clusters of people who fuse physical competence (food, water, repair, defense) with digital coordination (maps, communication, planning, reputation) and a pragmatic, low-ideology posture.
- Trust must be mediated by systems that are not purely tribal — reputation graphs, shared ledgers, identity schemes robust to capture by any single warlord or faction.
- Some check on purely predatory strategies must exist, so that raw extraction and terror are not the only viable path to power.

Equally, specific failure modes must be avoided. [hypothesis:C33] If every surviving cluster reverts to closed tribal or ideological fortresses, the result is not a new substrate — it is feudalism with better radios. If the surviving digital coordination layer is fully captured by pre-existing militaries, corporations, or cults, it is not a new substrate — it is a more efficient panopticon layered on the same coalition engine. And if shared reality disintegrates entirely — if no baseline consensus survives about what is true, where resources are, or what has actually happened — then no coordination physics, digital or otherwise, has enough common ground to take hold, and only noise and violence remain.

The honest prognosis, under the stated assumptions, is genuinely uncertain. Some of the necessary conditions are visibly forming; others are not. This is not cause for either complacency or despair — it is simply the nature of substrate transitions. They do not arrive pre-approved by trend lines. They arrive as ruptures, and their outcome is decided by whether a new coordination physics can establish itself faster than the old one disintegrates. [conjecture:C34]

## Part Four: Godhood, If We Earn It

### What "Becoming Gods" Actually Means

Set aside the mythological connotations. In this framework, **godhood** does not mean omnipotence or immortality. It means something more precise: the emergence of a coordination capacity that transcends the ancient limitations of the vertebrate coalition engine. [definition:C35] A species that can unify at planetary scale without requiring an enemy to hold it together. A civilization that can model and communicate not only within its own lineage but across cognitive architectures — bridging vertebrate mind-modeling and insect-style stigmergy into a single, coherent, hybrid coordination system.

This is what a successful molt produces: not a replacement of biological humanity, but an overlay — a digital coordination substrate that lets fish-people cognition finally escape the requirement of external conflict for internal coherence. [conjecture:C36] Gods, in this sense, are simply what fish people look like once they are no longer trapped inside an ecosystem economy that has already been won.

### Why the Insect Step Cannot Be Skipped

The temptation, when imagining this future, is to picture humans simply building bigger and better versions of the same mind-modeling institutions — larger councils, smarter parliaments, more empathetic diplomacy. But this piece's central claim is that this path cannot work, because it still runs entirely on vertebrate coalition logic, which structurally requires division to remain stable. [hypothesis:C37] No amount of scaling up mind-modeling institutions escapes the coalition trap; it only produces larger, more dangerous versions of the same tribal war.

The only coordination architecture on Earth that has ever demonstrated the ability to unify enormous numbers of agents *without* relying on modeling each other's beliefs, and *without* requiring an external enemy, is the insect model: stigmergic coordination through shared, legible, environmental signal. [interpretation:C38] This is precisely the logic that digital infrastructure is capable of implementing — transparent shared ledgers, open data, distributed sensing, systems where coordination emerges from the shared environment rather than from tribal narrative. [hypothesis:C39]

This is why the molt must run through an insect phase. [conjecture:C6] We are not becoming insects literally — we retain our recursive mind-modeling, our empathy, our narrative cognition, our theories about gods and physics alike. But functionally, our coordination infrastructure must adopt the insect solution to the problem the vertebrate lineage never solved: how to act as one coherent collective without needing to hate something to do it.

> *Fish people first became insect people once, long before there were fish at all — because stigmergy is, in the deepest sense, older and more general than mind-modeling.* [interpretation:C40]

Now, forced by the failure of our own coalition engine, we must consciously rebuild that older solution on top of our newer one. [normative:C28] The molt is not fish becoming gods directly. It is fish becoming insects, so that the resulting hybrid — narrative-capable, empathetic, mind-modeling, and simultaneously stigmergic, decentralized, and non-tribal — can finally become something genuinely new. [conjecture:C6]

## Conclusion

We began as fish, coordinating in schools through a lateral line that could feel the water move around it. That circuit mutated, again and again, into everything we call the social mind: empathy, negotiation, coalition, culture, superstition, science. It is a magnificent inheritance, and it is also a trap, because it cannot produce unity without an enemy, and it cannot model the other minds — insect, alien, or otherwise — that do not share its ancestry. [hypothesis:C3]

We are now living through the only event in vertebrate history that can break this trap: a substrate molt, arriving in the ugliest possible packaging — war, ecological catastrophe, economic collapse, political rupture. [conjecture:C5] Whether this becomes a true molt or merely a collapse depends on whether a new coordination physics, borrowed in function from the insects who solved this problem five hundred million years ago, can take root before the old vertebrate substrate finishes falling apart. [conjecture:C34]

If it does, what emerges will not be simply "digital humanity." It will be something that has never existed before: fish people who learned, at the last possible moment, to think like insects — and having done so, finally molted into something we have no better word for than gods. [conjecture:C36]

## Limits

This piece is a speculative synthesis, and several of its steps are weaker than its tone suggests. The lateral line was lost in adult land vertebrates, but aquatic amphibians retain it, and the claim that schooling is the single root of vertebrate social cognition is an inference rather than a demonstrated lineage. Rival accounts trace mentalizing and coalition behavior to primate-specific social pressures, not to a fish-level circuit.

The claim that insect coordination needs no external enemy is challenged by well-documented aggression between ant colonies, and insect cognition research has found more individual learning and flexibility than the phrase "hardwired templates" implies. Digital systems have also shown a strong tendency to amplify in-group and out-group dynamics, which cuts against the claim that they do not run on coalition logic.

The ~500-million-year figure fits the age of the vertebrate lineage. Insect eusociality is generally dated much more recently, so the Conclusion's phrase about insects solving this problem five hundred million years ago should be read loosely. The Part Three scenario is a stated assumption, not a forecast, and the five conditions and three failure modes are the author's judgment, not derived results.
